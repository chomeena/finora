GO DIGITAL

Simple digital steps that bring customers

About half of women business owners in one survey said getting started with digital tools was a hurdle. These steps are free or low cost.

Accept UPI payments

Get a QR code from your bank or a UPI app. Customers pay faster, and every payment becomes a record a lender can see.

WhatsApp Business catalogue

Show products, prices and photos in a free catalogue. Share the link with customers instead of typing the same message again and again.

Google Business Profile

List your business for free so people nearby can find your address, phone number and photos when they search.

Sell on marketplaces

The government’s Mahila e-Haat portal closed in 2020. The Ministry now points women sellers to the GeM and NSIC marketplaces. Private apps also exist.

Good photos sell

Use daylight and a plain background. Clear photos are one of the cheapest ways to look professional.

Stay safe online

Check payments in your own UPI or bank app, never from a screenshot. Try the Scam Spotter.

LEARN FROM OTHERS

Six mistakes beginners make

And how to avoid each one.

1. Mixing home money and business money

You cannot tell if the business is earning. Open a separate account and pay yourself a fixed amount each month.

2. Underpricing to win customers

Low prices feel safe but can hide losses. Include materials, packaging, transport and your own time. Use the Pricing Calculator.

3. Not keeping records

Banks and schemes ask for proof of income. Writing down every sale and expense takes five minutes a day. Try the Money Record Book.

4. Borrowing before knowing the numbers

Work out the EMI and your monthly profit first. See the EMI Calculator. Avoid informal lenders who charge very high interest.

5. Paying agents to get a scheme or loan

Government schemes are applied for directly at banks and on official portals. Be careful with anyone who asks for money to guarantee approval.

6. Waiting for the perfect time

Start small, sell to a few people and improve. Many successful businesses began with one customer.
